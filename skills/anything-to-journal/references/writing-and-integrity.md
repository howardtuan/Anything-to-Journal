# Evidence-led journal writing

## Write from evidence, not from a preferred story

Create a section evidence map before prose. Map each result, method, interpretation, citation, figure, table, and equation to stable source-material IDs and exact locators. Reconcile conflicts explicitly. Synthesize repetition, but retain the research question, design, population or materials, procedures, outcomes, negative and null findings, uncertainty, limitations, and contribution.

Preserve exactly:

- sample sizes, exclusions, dates, locations, groups, doses, durations, and instruments;
- signs, decimal places, units, ranges, intervals, p-values, effect sizes, and test names;
- causal versus correlational language and observed versus hypothesized claims;
- ethics identifiers, consent conditions, registrations, funding, and conflicts;
- names, taxonomies, standards, dataset/model versions, and equation semantics.

When sources disagree, do not silently choose one. Record the conflict and ask the author when it affects the manuscript.

## Control claim strength

- Use *shows* or *demonstrates* only when the design directly warrants it.
- Render possibility as *may* or *might*.
- Keep associations as associations, not causes.
- State a non-significant result as such; do not rewrite it as evidence of no effect.
- Do not add novelty, generalizability, mechanisms, clinical value, policy implications, data, or literature absent from the source set.

Every quantitative prose claim needs one adjacent evidence marker and one matching row in `manuscript/evidence-map.csv`.

## Produce clear journal English

- Build a terminology table from source definitions and field-standard usage.
- Choose one English term per concept and apply it consistently.
- Prefer direct subjects and verbs, short evidence-led paragraphs, and explicit logical links.
- Translate non-English material by meaning while preserving claim strength, values, and proper names.
- Define abbreviations once and use SI units unless field rules require otherwise.
- Keep author names in the author's established spelling or romanization; never invent names.
- Use a 2em first-line indent and 0pt paragraph spacing unless the confirmed official template requires otherwise.

Remove administrative prose, duplicated background, and repeated summaries only when no unique evidence or citation is lost. Structure the paper for its research design instead of forcing every project into IMRaD.

## Handle figures and tables

Translate captions and in-figure labels faithfully. For an English derivative, verify every plot coordinate, curve, bar, symbol, legend association, axis scale, unit, number, and color against the original. Retain the exact original asset unchanged and record the derivative's provenance.

Translate table prose without changing numbers, topology, notes, or citations. Re-run numeric parity after every edit. Place each used visual in the manuscript beside its substantive callout; put figure captions below visuals and table captions above cells.

## Preserve provenance and rights

Identify prior publications and third-party content in every source type. Do not assume public availability grants republication rights. Record author decisions on permissions, credit lines, author roles, ethics, consent, data/code availability, confidential or patented material, and prior publication.

Venue rules for generative-AI use change. Check the current target policy, prepare an accurate disclosure if required, and never list an AI system as an author. Human authors remain accountable for all text, data, citations, permissions, and final submission.
